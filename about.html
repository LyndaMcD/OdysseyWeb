Project Odyssey

An alternate reality game for library orientation

Project Odyssey is a student-led alternate reality game designed to explore new ways of introducing students to university libraries.

Instead of a traditional library orientation, players follow a trail of clues, puzzles, websites and physical artefacts placed throughout the library. As the mystery unfolds, players are encouraged to explore the space, interact with library materials, search for information and become more familiar with the library through play.

The project is designed around the idea that library orientation can be something students experience rather than simply being told about. By embedding puzzles into the physical and digital environment, Project Odyssey aims to encourage exploration, curiosity and confidence while reducing some of the uncertainty that can come with navigating an unfamiliar academic library.

Designed to travel

Project Odyssey is being developed as a reusable framework rather than a game tied to a single building.

Participating libraries can adapt parts of the puzzle trail to their own collections, spaces and resources while keeping the central narrative and digital components intact. This allows each version of the game to feel connected to its host library without requiring an entirely new ARG to be created from scratch.

Research

Project Odyssey is also a research project investigating how alternate reality games can be used for library orientation and student engagement.

0 The project examines players' experiences of exploring the library through the game, including their confidence using the space, their experience of the puzzles, and their willingness to explore and experiment during play.

Project Odyssey was developed at the University of Malta as part of the SEA-EU student-led project programme.
